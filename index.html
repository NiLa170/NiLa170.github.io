San Francisco Crime Story

Why Did Larceny/Theft Drop So Sharply from 2019 to 2020?

San Francisco's crime data presents the development of different crime types over time. The dataset has been reduced to a subset of categories named Personal Focus Crimes, which include various types of crimes such as assault, robbery, and larceny/ theft. Among these, larceny/ theft stands out as the most frequent crime, and showing the most interesting development over the last two decades. Particularly, the biggest one-year break in the series lines up with the first COVID-19 shutdown year 2020.

1) The Pattern We Want to Explain

The number of larency/ theft incidents falls from 44,749 incidents in 2019 to 28,727 in 2020, a 35.8% drop. This change is very outstanding, so the question is: what changed in the city that year?

2) Where Theft Is Concentrated

The map summarizes where theft has historically concentrated. Hotspots cluster in central commercial and visitor-heavy areas, consistent with places that usually have high foot traffic, parked cars, shopping, and tourism. In a normal year, these are high-opportunity environments for larceny.

3) How Daily Timing Changed

The hourly animation shows that 2020 is not only lower in total volume, but it also shifts the daily profile. Before 2020, incidents are mainly concentrated in the second half of the day, with peaks in the early evening. In 2020, however, incidents are more evenly distributed across these hours. One way to read this is that routine daytime movement patterns were disrupted, and theft opportunities changed with them.

4) Possible Causes of the 2020 Drop

The data cannot prove one single cause, but the timing and external evidence point to a strong multi-factor explanation:

Cause A: Sudden mobility restrictions. San Francisco's Health Officer order dated March 16, 2020 required residents to shelter in place, directed non-essential business operations to stop at physical locations, and restricted non-essential travel. This starts March 17, 2020, exactly when our monthly counts begin to collapse.

Cause B: Fewer commuters and visitors in the city core. MTC transit statistics show weekday BART exits plunging from 409,515 (March 2019) to 166,574 (March 2020), and from 414,397 (April 2019) to 25,136 (April 2020). SF Travel also reports that San Francisco's daily visitors averaged 71,805 in 2019 versus 32,281 in 2020. Fewer people and fewer parked/visited areas mean fewer easy theft opportunities.

Cause C: A broader property-crime downturn in 2020. FBI national statistics report a decline in property crime (and specifically larceny-theft) from 2019 to 2020. That broader background trend is consistent with what we observe locally in SF.

There is also a compositional clue inside the local data: Theft, From Locked Vehicle, >$950 drops from 18,769 in 2019 to 9,695 in 2020 (-48.3%). Because theft from vehicles is a major share of SF larceny, this single subtype helps explain much of the total decline.

5) What We Can and Cannot Conclude

The safest conclusion is not "crime suddenly got solved in 2020." A better conclusion is that routine activity changed dramatically: travel collapsed, public space use shifted, and opportunity structures for theft changed. As reopening progressed, the series partially rebounded in 2021 and 2022, which is also what we would expect from an opportunity-based explanation.

Finally, this is police-reported incident data, so it reflects both behavior and reporting/enforcement patterns. That limitation matters whenever we interpret year-to-year changes.
